## Summary

My product work was spread across many tools, and gathering the information took more time than using it. I built Arnie OS: 11 AI agents that read from those tools, draft PRDs, reports and replies, and file the results. I approve what goes out, so my time goes on conversations and decisions.

## Context

As Product Director at PVcase, my week mixed research, PRDs, reporting, team 1:1s and a heavy meeting load. Each of those needed information from a different set of tools.

I started building Arnie OS in April 2026 to take on the repetitive parts. It is a set of agents that each own one kind of work. One agent, Oprah, acts as chief of staff. I built it in stages: build one part, use it, then adjust.

## My role

As Product Director at PVcase, I designed, built and operate Arnie OS. I defined each agent's role, the tools it can read, what it can do independently and what requires my approval. I use it in my own work, and I have shared the agent documentation with my team so they can use it too.

## Decisions and trade-offs

Six decisions shaped how Arnie OS works. Each agent has a name and one job, and I introduce them as they appear.

- **Drafts wait for my approval.** Wade (communication), Eminem (PRDs and reports) and Tony (prototypes) do not send or file their work until I approve it. I trained Wade, the agent that drafts my messages, on accuracy and on my tone of voice, and approval is how I corrected him. The trade-off is that I stay in the loop on every outgoing message.
- **One chief of staff routes every escalation.** Oprah, the chief of staff, filters and prioritises. Every agent escalates to her first, and her three tiers decide what happens: queue it, flag it today or interrupt me. The trade-off is that everything passes through one agent. In return, I am interrupted only when something needs me.
- **One agent files.** Mary, the knowledge organiser, is the only agent that files to Slite. With a single filer, filing mistakes are easy to catch.
- **Confidence labels on analysis.** Lisa, the analyst, labels her metrics as validated, directional or insufficient, so I can see how far to trust a number and what it came from. The labels also help me train her.
- **Confidential sources stay unconnected.** Productboard and Culture Amp hold confidential customer and team information. I did not connect them to the agents, so nothing from them can leak.
- **Build in stages.** I built one part, used it, learned what it needed and adjusted. That kept me from over-engineering the system before I knew what I needed.

## What we shipped

Together, the agents take over the gathering, drafting and chasing that used to fill my week. They read from the tools I already use, so the information I used to hunt for arrives ready to use. Research agents keep customer, market and roadmap evidence current, so I stop missing the important signals. Writing agents turn that evidence into PRDs, reports and replies in my voice. A planning agent keeps my week and my team work on track. One chief of staff watches everything, decides what reaches me and protects my focus. Each agent has one job:

## Outcome

Arnie OS changed how I work, not only how fast I work. My information stays up to date and deadlines no longer slip. I stay ahead on market and customer insight. The time I used to spend gathering and formatting now goes into conversations with colleagues, customers and stakeholders, and into decisions I can support with evidence.

I can now give any task my full focus. I know my agents are working in the background on the other topics I am responsible for, and that Oprah will interrupt me only when something needs me.

I used it to take the AI-assisted design review from idea to shipped in under a month.
